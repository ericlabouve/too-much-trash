Open triangular guides · three interpretations

Orange = bracket · teal = moving plunger · the raised tail handle travels in the continuous top slot.
These are schematic guide sections, not regenerated V3 parts. Mount, pulley, shoe, band anchors and travel stops are omitted to make the alternatives clear.

A · Point-down V cradle

A triangular neck points down into a matching V. The whole top stays open for the handle.

Simplest and lightest interpretation. Guides sideways motion and rotation, but the plunger can lift straight out. It would need separate retention.

Support strategy: open cradle; print guide and plunger along their length, standing on their end faces.

B · Point-up captured triangle

A wide-bottom triangular neck slides into a matching triangular tunnel. A narrow continuous slot replaces the apex so the tail handle can pass.

Closest to your description; recommended. The broad base cannot lift through the slot. Insert the plunger from an open end.

Support strategy: print the guide standing on an end face, so the retaining walls grow vertically rather than overhang.

C · Captured diamond neck

Two triangles form a diamond-shaped neck. Lower slopes center it; upper slopes capture it. The top apex opens into the handle slot.

More guidance, potentially more friction. Angled faces constrain lift and sideways movement; clearance on more faces becomes sensitive.

Support strategy: end-face printing, as in B. A deeper section may offset any material savings.

All three: a continuous longitudinal opening lets the handle travel. B and C resist lifting out but still need an end stop to prevent sliding out lengthwise. A small removable stop could keep assembly possible.
