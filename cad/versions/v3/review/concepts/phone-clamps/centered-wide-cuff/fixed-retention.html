Same cuff. Two ways to avoid the neck.

All three cuffs remain the same 21.6 mm part. Both opposite cuffs keep their existing crossed-T locks. Only the fixed jaw attachment differs below; no cuff widening, offset or phone shift.

1 · Integral T-stud

No loose fixed-retainer parts

The stud is printed as part of the jaw and points into the cuff, away from the neck. Its tapered head sits in the existing recess.

The TPU must flex over the head. That deformation is not simulated here. Do not force the PETG cuff over this stud. Pull-off resistance, peel and creep need physical TPU tests.

2 · Short T + sideways keeper

Removable lock, but a tight package

An 8.6 mm T replaces the longer sliding-cuff T here. The blue keeper is the exact existing 12.2 mm axle key, turned sideways with its broad head oriented away from the neck.

The T stops 0.5 mm before the neck. Its tip ligament and the receiver's front channel wall are only 1.1 mm: not strength-qualified. Remove the phone, pull the keeper sideways, then pull the T toward the phone. The keeper itself can still drift.

Exploded spacing illustrates separation, not a verified deforming assembly animation. In Full assembly mode, only assembled positions are shown. The silver neck can be hidden to inspect the short T. Cuff and jaw geometry are not scaled to fake clearance.

Validation scope

Both alternatives are review-only and unprinted. CAD checks cover valid solids, nominal assembled intersections with the neck/cuff/current bare-near actuator, and rigid pull-out obstruction. Option 2 additionally samples its local keeper and T removal paths at 0.5 mm steps. These checks do not establish strength, TPU snap-fit force, comfortable finger access, tolerance robustness, all-phone button clearance, screen/camera access or resistance to vibration. Existing collar/twine/full-travel issues remain unresolved.
