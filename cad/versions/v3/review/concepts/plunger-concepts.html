Recommendation: A1, with room for optional A4 return leaves

A1 keeps the pull on the plunger axis and uses a rotating wheel to turn the cord. Use a captive keyed slider, two separated bearing lands with relieved space between them, a cord-retaining wheel cover and accessible clean-out openings. These are proposed design features, not validated dimensions. The side projection places the wheel in front of the slider in depth; that packaging must be checked in CAD.

Can the phone button provide the return?

Possibly. Throughout release, its restoring force must exceed slider stiction, cord and axle resistance, any residual trigger tension, and gravity opposing return. Button return force has not been measured. A button can push the plunger back to its released height, but cannot push it farther to establish an air gap. A captive, nearly unloaded contact may work; guaranteed clearance calls for an independent return source such as A4. A tension-only cord going slack does not positively retract the plunger.

The 2:1 A3 tackle uses twice the cord travel per unit of plunger travel and approximately doubles force in an ideal frictionless model. It does not solve arbitrary trigger overtravel, and return must back-drive both wheels. It is a stroke-reduction option, not a force-limiting option.

Do not obtain extra stroke by compressing a spring directly against the phone indefinitely. A frame stop alone also cannot absorb the remaining motion of a rigidly coupled trigger.

What makes the compact version robust?

  • Center the cord attachment over the button reaction to reduce tipping. Avoid an offset arm like the original A sketch.
  • Use one keyed slider rather than two tightly fitted parallel rails. Relieve non-bearing surfaces for print variation and debris clearance; reduced contact area alone is not a guarantee of lower friction.
  • Print guide faces and the slider without support scars on mating surfaces; assemble separately rather than print in place.
  • Use a broad radiused cord groove and a captive wheel/axle. Select the smallest wheel only after checking the actual twine bending and axle drag; no textile-twine diameter is yet known.
  • Keep the contact face limited to the intended button; the previous 16 mm shoe should not automatically transfer to this mechanism.
  • A4 leaves should be replaceable and near unloaded at rest where practical. Their fatigue life and creep are unknown.

Evidence and limits

Sources below inform the mechanism principles. They do not establish this phone's force/travel, the proposed print clearances, or a finished module envelope. No source found here provides a verified return-force curve for the user's phone.

  • igus: eccentric drive loads and binding — Keep the drive near the guide axis and avoid overconstraining parallel guides. Their numeric 2:1 rule is specific to their bearings, not a PLA design limit.
  • OMRON: tactile switch precautions — Straight actuation and avoidance of excess force after the switch stops. General design guidance only: not an iPhone switch specification.
  • MIT: capstan friction — Cord sliding around a fixed surface incurs friction; unknown twine/PLA friction prevents a numerical return prediction.
  • Sava: pulley design — Pulley bend diameter matters. Published wire-rope diameter ratios must not be transferred to unspecified textile twine.
  • Sharkey et al.: printed flexure translation stage — Printed flexures are a demonstrated mechanism family. This does not validate these leaf dimensions or lifetime.
  • Fischbach and Weinberg: flexural creep of printed PLA — PLA flexural creep and aging are experimentally relevant; permanent spring preload needs validation.
