Shorter clamp with matching pads

Two 3 × 2 mm elongated hexagonal passages guide the nominal 1 mm string through the cap’s handle-end screw bosses. They leave 1.35 mm to the screw bores. The openings retain 45° roofs; a fresh PETG slice confirms zero support and interface material with automatic supports enabled. Threading, strength and string wear remain untested.

Each screw-bearing end moves inward 5 mm. Clamp length is 97 mm, with 80 mm between screw pairs. The pads and their window lips follow the shorter openings. The fixed cuff and integral T stud sit 3.28 mm lower, centered at Z 8 mm with the plunger foot. The adjacent clamp opening and pad lip are shortened to retain the stud backing. The separate slot in the fixed-jaw arm extends 14 mm toward the clamp at 6 mm width, removing 336 mm³ of solid CAD material. Both shaft-cap rail projections are shortened 30%, from 46.2 to 32.34 mm. Slot end walls remain 2.6 mm thick. The displayed actuator fits; the large-phone far-side position exceeds the shorter adjustment range. The cap has now been sliced with automatic supports enabled and requires zero support material. The revised fixed jaw still needs slicing; physical checks remain pending.

Each pad follows its own rigid face and has matching through-cutouts. Nominal TPU contact area is 853 mm² on the cap and 1,289 mm² on the fixed jaw. A continuous lip projects 1.6 mm into each rigid window, one on the cap and two on the fixed jaw.

Each pad replaces 0.8 mm of rigid material at the contact face. The original shaft opening is preserved. The cap recess opens away from the print bed, and the fixed jaw retains a flat bed face.

The geometry avoids a blind pocket over the print bed. Verify support requirements in the slicer and check lip fit on the printed parts.

V4 design queue

  1. Harness slip. String pull moves the clamp toward the handle, and clamping force fades in use.
  2. String tension. Harness movement creates slack, and dialing in tension is difficult.
  3. Pulley derailment. The string leaves the pulley. Explore guides integrated into either side of the fixed jaw.
  4. Actuator travel and fit. Add a plunger stop, center the contact on the volume button, and move the handle to meet the end of the actuator rail.
  5. Fixed jaw cuff and camera aim. Explore moving the single T stud back about 2.5 mm to improve button alignment and bring the claws closer to the center of the video.
  6. Shaft cap and fixed jaw length. Shorten both clamp bodies along the shaft to reduce material.
  7. Shaft cap rails. Shorten each of the two actuator rails by 30%.
