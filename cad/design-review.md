# R4 design review — fit prototype, not field-qualified

R4 implements the user-selected Option B: phone beside shaft, centered gripping band, rear support, either-side actuator, and centered trigger-facing housing routing. It retains six unique printed designs / seven pieces. Solid volume is 91.69 cm³, down from 103.19 cm³ in R3 (11.1%); actual sliced mass is not measured. R3 is recoverable from tag `cad-r3-first-draft`; all original concept PNGs remain intact. The selected negative-X placement supersedes the earlier positive-X concept offset. The measured 14 ×19 mm shaft supersedes the square-section assumption.

## Mechanism credibility and remaining limits

- **Defined reaction path:** both housing ends have ferrule shoulders and inner-wire bores. At the phone, bracket → pivot → rocker → locked soft contact screw carries the load; the phone clamp reacts the button force. The handle stop reacts housing compression while the strap follows the moving trigger.
- **Excess travel:** a series extension spring absorbs stroke after the adjustable hard stop engages. A torsion spring returns the rocker against its release ledge. Continued squeezing therefore does not require additional button displacement. This depends on correctly set stops, spring rating and sufficiently stiff printed supports.
- **Force budget:** assuming an aligned upper-trigger cable tie, 20/40 mm axial trigger movement produces 20/40 mm take-up. Approximately 1 mm drives the rocker, leaving up to 39 mm extension. Provisional spring initial tension 1.5 N plus 0.055 N/mm gives 3.64 N cable tension and about 5.19 N available tip force before the stop takes excess load. These are **not safe phone force ratings**. Actual trigger path, friction, button force and support compliance require measurement. Source and measure a suitable spring before final printing.
- **Contact path:** the nominal 0.65 mm inward movement is accompanied by about 0.66 mm movement across the button face. Check the complete contact patch throughout this arc; a guided shoe may be necessary if the measured button cannot tolerate it.
- **Retention:** rear ledges and narrow front catches leave the screen clear, but thin phones have clearance to the front catches. Padded side friction therefore carries axial and some out-of-plane loads. No axial end stop is included in this centered adjustable design. Use a separate tether; dummy-phone tests were declined; physical retention remains unverified. Never compensate for poor retention by excessive clamp force. Wallet cards alter thickness and stiffness.
- **Balance:** the phone midpoint is aligned to the gripping band. The phone remains laterally offset from the shaft as explicitly selected; the assembly is not mass-balanced around the shaft. Check wrist torque and saddle slip with the actual phone mass.
- **Access:** jaw band is centered along phone length, below the sampled volume-up location; the actuator slides along the edge and in height. Camera keep-out and phone solids are simple envelopes, not exact device models. All camera/microphone/port positions, glass-edge contacts and case features require inspection. The iPhone 14–18 target is an intent, not established compatibility.
- **Stock tool:** only the shaft cross-section, available 200 mm region and approximate trigger stroke are confirmed. The stock silhouette, handle station and cable routing are provisional. The conflicting earlier trigger coordinates are not used as validated geometry. Strap creep, finger clearance, actual housing bends and full claw clearance must be tested.

## Verification performed by scripts

`source/check_design.py` checks the two supplied sample envelopes plus two provisional adjustment limits, each with both actuator sides, printed-part intersections, rocker sweep through eleven positions per setting, selected metal hardware clearance, zero shaft/phone overlap, centered gripping band, analytic travel and the proposed spring extension allowance. `source/build.py` requires a valid connected B-rep for each part and watertight positive-volume STL exports. Outputs are in `print/validation.json` and `print/manifest.json`. These checks do not constitute FEA, fatigue analysis, slicer approval, spring qualification, or physical testing.

## Physical checks still outstanding (dummy-phone testing declined)

1. Fit a short collar sample/shims to the actual fluted shaft and confirm slip resistance without tube deformation. Fit a slider/nut-pocket sample to the intended printer and purchased hardware.
2. Assemble the actuator off the phone with actual springs, pivot and cable. Measure pull distance/force and verify that the stop carries excess load while the extension spring stays within its rated range over the entire 40 mm trigger stroke. Check release, rubbing, wire clamping and accidental knocks.
3. Confirm at least 100 squeeze/release cycles at different housing bends, including incomplete squeezes and full grip closure. Reject sticking, strap migration, cable fraying or permanent spring extension.
4. Dummy-phone testing is omitted at the user’s request. Actual phone/case fit, jaw retention and collar slip remain unverified; use conservative contact adjustment and the independent tether.
5. Inspect CAD-oriented STLs in Bambu Studio, paint local supports as documented, and inspect sliced nut pockets, ferrule shoulders and layer direction. Record material, measured clearances and slicer mass/time; these have not been physically validated.
6. Verify the iOS app handles a held volume-up press as intended. Hardware holding a button is not proof of recording/event behavior.

Manufacturability status: editable, exportable and designed for common FDM processes with conventional hardware. Final suitability awaits the tests above; especially source the unusually low-rate extension spring and confirm phone force before a final set.

## Hardware detail review and first fit print

The second hardware visualization pass exposed a carrier interference with the full 25 mm actuator bolt. The BOM now calls for standard M3×20 carriage bolts, leaving about 2.6 mm beyond the modeled nut. Confirm the actual washer/nut stack; no trimming is required for the illustrated stack. Static carriage-fastener and phone-ferrule clearance are now included in the eight-configuration checks. The ferrule/housing also revealed an obstructed entry beside the lower actuator plate. Extending the 5.6 mm entry bore down through that plate clears the route while preserving the reaction shoulder and at least 3.8 mm backing thickness. A 9.6 mm wide housing boss clears the 6 mm OD washers at low adjustment positions, retaining 2 mm walls around the 5.6 mm bore. A small rounded notch at the carrier guide end clears the far-side fasteners on narrow phones. Carrier and actuator-bracket prints changed; other four designs are unchanged.

The CAD is sufficient to begin a staged PLA/PLA+ fit print after slicer review. Start with neck cap, rocker and actuator bracket; then print the remaining rigid pieces after checking their hardware interfaces. Cosmetic render refinement is not a readiness gate. Actual spring sourcing, friction/retention, stop calibration and the lateral contact sweep remain gates for reliable phone operation. No physical test or Bambu Studio slice has been performed.
