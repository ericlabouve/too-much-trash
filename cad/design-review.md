# R3 design review — fit prototype, not field-qualified

R3 replaces eleven printed pieces with seven and removes independent length shoes, the broad carrier ladder and the long handle outrigger. A common rectangular cap is reused twice. Total solid CAD volume falls from 132.89 cm³ in the archived R2 STLs to 103.19 cm³ in R3 (22.3% lower); actual sliced mass also depends on supports, walls and infill. The fixed saddle, carrier rail and jaw are integrated; the moving jaw uses a metal draw screw. The concept PNGs remain intact. Measured 14 ×19 mm shaft geometry supersedes their square-section assumption. One consistent phone offset matches the intended positive-X / negative-project-Z placement rather than attempting to reproduce inconsistent front/side illustrations.

## Mechanism credibility and remaining limits

- **Defined reaction path:** both housing ends have ferrule shoulders and inner-wire bores. At the phone, bracket → pivot → rocker → locked soft contact screw carries the load; the phone clamp reacts the button force. The handle stop reacts housing compression while the strap follows the moving trigger.
- **Excess travel:** a series extension spring absorbs stroke after the adjustable hard stop engages. A torsion spring returns the rocker against its release ledge. Continued squeezing therefore does not require additional button displacement. This depends on correctly set stops, spring rating and sufficiently stiff printed supports.
- **Force budget:** illustrative anchor geometry gives 17.2/35.1 mm cable take-up for 20/40 mm trigger travel. Approximately 1 mm drives the rocker. Proposed series-spring initial tension 1.5 N + 0.055 N/mm × remaining extension yields ~3.37 N at full squeeze. After modeled return torque, the available tip force is roughly 4.7–4.8 N before the stop reacts excess load. This is **not a safe phone force rating**; actual button force, friction, case stiffness and bracket compliance are unmeasured. A weaker spring may fail to press; a stronger one raises load and deflection. Select and measure a real spring before final printing.
- **Contact path:** the rocker tip advances 0.65 mm under the nominal gap/stroke setting and rises roughly 0.43–0.79 mm depending on thickness. The soft tip must stay centered on the button throughout that path. No claim of purely linear pressing is made. Verify button-face size and lateral scrub on the sample; if unacceptable, a guided shoe will be needed.
- **Retention:** V jaws positively oppose out-of-plane escape and one end stop blocks motion toward the charging end. Motion toward the camera end depends on pad friction. Use a tether; test axial pull and inversion with a dummy phone mass. Do not compensate for bad retention by crushing the phone. Wallet card loads can change thickness and stiffness.
- **Access:** jaw band is below the sampled volume-up location; the actuator slides along the edge and in height. Camera keep-out and phone solids are simple envelopes, not exact device models. All camera/microphone/port positions, glass-edge contacts and case features require inspection. The iPhone 14–18 target is an intent, not established compatibility.
- **Stock tool:** only the shaft cross-section, available 200 mm region and approximate trigger stroke are confirmed. The stock silhouette, handle station and cable routing are provisional. The conflicting earlier trigger coordinates are not used as validated geometry. Strap creep, finger clearance, actual housing bends and full claw clearance must be tested.

## Verification performed by scripts

`source/check_design.py` checks the two supplied sample envelopes plus adjustment extremes, printed-part intersections, rocker sweep through eleven positions per setting, selected metal hardware clearance, analytic travel and the proposed spring extension allowance. `source/build.py` requires a valid connected B-rep for each part and watertight positive-volume STL exports. Outputs are in `print/validation.json` and `print/manifest.json`. These checks do not constitute FEA, fatigue analysis, slicer approval, spring qualification, or physical testing.

## Required prototype sequence

1. Fit a short collar sample/shims to the actual fluted shaft and confirm slip resistance without tube deformation. Fit a slider/nut-pocket sample to the intended printer and purchased hardware.
2. Assemble the actuator off the phone with actual springs, pivot, cable and dummy button gauge. Measure pull distance/force and verify that the stop carries excess load while the extension spring stays within its rated range over the entire 40 mm trigger stroke. Check release, rubbing, wire clamping and accidental knocks.
3. Confirm at least 100 squeeze/release cycles at different housing bends, including incomplete squeezes and full grip closure. Reject sticking, strap migration, cable fraying or permanent spring extension.
4. Clamp a dummy phone of the measured mass with the actual pads. Invert, shake, and apply axial loads; inspect jaw opening, creep, guide flex, charging stop and collar slip. Then test actual phone/case gently with tether and conservative contact adjustment.
5. Inspect CAD-oriented STLs in Bambu Studio, paint local supports as documented, and inspect sliced nut pockets, ferrule shoulders and layer direction. Record material, measured clearances and slicer mass/time; these have not been physically validated.
6. Verify the iOS app handles a held volume-up press as intended. Hardware holding a button is not proof of recording/event behavior.

Manufacturability status: editable, exportable and designed for common FDM processes with conventional hardware. Final suitability awaits the tests above; especially source the unusually low-rate extension spring and confirm phone force before a final set.
