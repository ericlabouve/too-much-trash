# R2 design review and physical validation

The model is mechanically constrained and printable, but the available stock reference is a single front photo with no scale. It cannot prove the actual neck profile, claw sweep, trigger trajectory, or cable stroke. The older `renders/` sheets are illustrations and disagree about phone translation between their a and b views. This CAD adopts one phone offset, a split square collar, and an open carrier with independent width, length and button-position slides. The blue handle and claw proxies in the full rendering are not measured factory geometry.

1. **Fit and retention:** Caliper the neck on four faces at both collar stations, including ribs. Measure every intended phone/case and camera bump. The 66–82 × 135–165 × 7–12 mm adjustment range is provisional, and cases can obscure buttons or ports. Print a short collar section and phone corner gauge first. Verify equal-height underside shims, edge-only lip pressure, port/mic/camera clearance, and that both camera-end shoes stay locked under inversion, shake and a gentle pull. The fixed maximum-width crossbars protrude beyond narrower phones; assess snag/claw clearance. Use a tether during initial use.
2. **Cable kinematics:** Record stationary anchor and trigger-tab coordinates with the trigger open and fully squeezed. Measure the exposed-wire length change; it must increase and exceed the lever's required input travel by a margin, without stopping claw closure. Measure cable friction on the routed housing, especially after bends and repeated flexing.
3. **Button loading:** Measure rocker location, force, and travel on each exact iPhone/case. Slide and lock the actuator carriage at each phone's button Y, then set the contact screw in the vertical slot and lock both nuts. The illustrative 0.45 mm `button_travel` is **not** a safe rated stroke. Adjust contact gap and hard stop on a dummy block, then start with the stop backed off. Check return after at least 100 cycles, impact bumps, grip changes, and cable slack variation at the travel extremes. Verify the iOS recording behavior independently; hardware pressing a rocker does not guarantee the application marks a grasp.
4. **Structure:** Test the collar against slip/rotation without crushing the thin stock tube. Pull-test the carrier wing, outboard handle stop, cable nipple keeper, pivot, and trigger ties. Confirm no printed part enters the full claw motion envelope at any wrist angle; preserve at least the measured safe gap, provisionally 25 mm. A dropped phone is the main field hazard.
5. **Printing:** PETG is the baseline for clips and lever; ASA is suitable if heat/UV exposure warrants it and the printer can control warping. Avoid PLA in sun or near a hot car. Use soft TPU/rubber only as replaceable pads/shims/contact cap, not as the structural clamp. A metal pivot and wire are deliberate wear/tension elements. Deburr all phone-contact lips and inspect layer bonding and first-layer fit. Consumer FDM hole sizes vary: drill/ream screw and pivot bores to fit if necessary.

Do not use this assembly as a final field fixture until these checks pass. Within the stated adjustment range, change the target phone parameters to regenerate the STEP/drawings; the printable STLs stay the same. Outside that range, revise the limits and print a new set. Record each actual phone model/case, rail/shoe/carriage settings, pad thickness, printer, filament, hardware, and measured cable/button travel.
