# R1 design review and physical validation

The model is mechanically constrained and printable, but the available stock reference is a single front photo with no scale. It cannot prove the actual neck profile, claw sweep, trigger trajectory, or cable stroke. The older `renders/` sheets are illustrations and disagree about phone translation between their a and b views. This CAD adopts one phone offset, a split square collar, and an open adjustable carrier. No model of the factory handle internals or claw curvature is claimed.

1. **Fit and retention:** Caliper the neck on four faces at both collar stations, including ribs. Measure the phone/case and camera bump. Print a short collar section and phone corner gauge first. Verify pad pressure is on edges only, port/mics/cameras/buttons are unobstructed, and the assembled phone cannot escape under inversion, shake, or a gentle pull. Use a tether during initial use.
2. **Cable kinematics:** Record stationary anchor and trigger-tab coordinates with the trigger open and fully squeezed. Measure the exposed-wire length change; it must increase and exceed the lever's required input travel by a margin, without stopping claw closure. Measure cable friction on the routed housing, especially after bends and repeated flexing.
3. **Button loading:** Measure rocker location, force, and travel on the exact iPhone/case. The illustrative 0.45 mm `button_travel` is **not** a safe rated stroke. Adjust contact gap and hard stop on a dummy block, then start with the stop backed off. Check return after at least 100 cycles, impact bumps, grip changes, and cable slack variation. Verify the iOS recording behavior independently; hardware pressing a rocker does not guarantee the application marks a grasp.
4. **Structure:** Test the collar against slip/rotation without crushing the thin stock tube. Pull-test the carrier wing, outboard handle stop, cable nipple keeper, pivot, and trigger ties. Confirm no printed part enters the full claw motion envelope at any wrist angle; preserve at least the measured safe gap, provisionally 25 mm. A dropped phone is the main field hazard.
5. **Printing:** PETG is the baseline for clips and lever; ASA is suitable if heat/UV exposure warrants it and the printer can control warping. Avoid PLA in sun or near a hot car. Use soft TPU/rubber only as replaceable pads/shims/contact cap, not as the structural clamp. A metal pivot and wire are deliberate wear/tension elements. Deburr all phone-contact lips and inspect layer bonding and first-layer fit. Consumer FDM hole sizes vary: drill/ream screw and pivot bores to fit if necessary.

Do not use this assembly as a final field fixture until these checks pass. Revise the parameter file and export new STL/STEP files after measurements and prototype results; record the actual phone model, printer, filament, hardware, and measured cable/button travel with that revision.
